Chip also takes every attribute of `<span>`, for example `title` and `aria-label`.

## When to use

- To mark a record with a short word: `admin`, `blocked`, `task`.
- For a count beside a heading, in `mono`: see [SectionLabel](?path=/docs/board-sectionlabel--docs).

## When not to use

- For an action. A chip is not a button: use [Button](?path=/docs/core-button--docs) with `variant="ghost" size="sm"`.
- For a state an agent changes live. Use [StatusDot](?path=/docs/core-statusdot--docs).

<DoDont>
<Do>

- Keep it to one or two words.
- Use `mono` for machine words: ids, roles, counts.
- Pick a tone for its meaning, the same way on every screen: `danger` for blocked, `green` for done.

</Do>
<Dont>

- Put a sentence in a chip. It wraps badly and reads as a button.
- Use tones to decorate. Five colours in one row mean nothing.

</Dont>
</DoDont>

## Tones

<Canvas of={Stories.Tones} />

The top row is the sans face, the bottom row `mono`. `claim` is the yellow of a claimed ticket;
`neutral` is grey on the input fill.

## Accessibility

- A chip is plain text in a `<span>`. A screen reader reads its words and nothing of its colour, so
  the words must carry the meaning.
- A chip takes no focus and has no keys.

<Gap>Contrast: `danger` text on its own tint is 4.1:1, under AA (Tokens, "Contrast: pairs in use").</Gap>
